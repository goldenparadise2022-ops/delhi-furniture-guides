Placing a 3-Seater Sofa Under a Window: Sunlight and Heat Checklist

Sometimes the only long wall in a living room has a window. Placing a three-seater under it can work well, but Delhi summer sun, window sills and curtains need planning first. Use it alongside your own measurements and household routines rather than as a fixed rulebook.

Household checklist

  • Note which direction the window faces.
  • Measure sill height from the floor.
  • Check curtain stack width when open.
  • Leave a small gap between sofa back and wall.

Details worth getting right

Colour and fading

Strong sunlight can fade many fabrics over time. West-facing windows receive intense afternoon sun in May and June; a blind or sheer curtain protects both the sofa and the room temperature.

Keep the window usable

A sofa pushed tight against a low sill can make it hard to open the window or reach the latch. Leaving a small gap also lets curtains hang straight.

Delhi home context

Delhi homes vary widely, from compact DDA flats and builder floors to large NCR apartments. Measure the clear floor rather than trusting brochure areas, and note doors, switchboards, windows and AC units before you shortlist anything.

In a west-facing flat in Noida, drawing a sheer curtain between 2 pm and 5 pm can be a simple habit that protects a darker fabric.

Room shape, light and the way people move through the space matter as much as any single dimension. Sketch the room roughly to scale and pencil in the sofa, tables and walking routes before making a decision. A short written record of what you chose and why makes future cleaning, repairs or replacements much simpler.

Mistakes that are easy to make

  • Measuring the room once, in a hurry, and trusting the number.
  • Forgetting door swings, switchboards and the AC drain pipe.
  • Choosing the largest sofa the wall can hold instead of the one the room can breathe around.

Step-by-step approach

Measure twice on different days, compare with a sofa you already know, then tape the footprint on the floor. Only after the room passes that test should you start comparing specific sizes and styles. Write your final numbers on a single page and carry it when you visit showrooms or compare listings online.
