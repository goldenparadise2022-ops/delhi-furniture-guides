Sheesham, Mango, Teak and Rubberwood: Timber Comparison for Delhi Weather

Delhi furniture lives through dry summer heat, humid monsoon weeks and cool winters with heaters. The timber species, and how well it has been seasoned, affects how a piece handles those swings. This comparison focuses on the woods most commonly quoted in Indian furniture stores; always ask the seller to confirm the species and seasoning in writing.

Species at a glance

Sheesham (Indian rosewood)Dense, strong grainHeavyBeds, dining sets, sofasWell-seasoned pieces handle seasonal change well

Questions to ask the seller

  • Is the frame solid timber throughout, or only the visible surfaces?
  • Was the wood kiln-dried or air-seasoned, and for how long?
  • What finish is used: PU, melamine, oil or wax?
  • Is the underside and back sealed as well as the top?
  • What does the warranty cover if joints open or wood cracks?

Why seasoning matters more than the name

Poorly seasoned wood of a good species can still warp or crack after its first monsoon. A well-seasoned, well-sealed mid-range timber often outperforms it. Look at joints and end grain, and ask how long the wood was dried.

Common mistakes

  • Accepting “sheesham finish” as meaning solid sheesham.
  • Placing any solid wood piece directly against a damp external wall.
  • Choosing a heavy timber for furniture that must go up four floors without a lift.
