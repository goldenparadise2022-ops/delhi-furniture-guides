Upholstery Cleaning Code Table: W, S, WS and X Explained

Many sofas carry a cleaning code on the care label. Knowing what it means prevents well-meant cleaning from damaging the fabric of a three-seater. Keep a pen handy: the table and checklist are meant to be filled in and compared, not just read.

At-a-glance chart

CodeCommon meaningPractical approach
WWater-based cleaningMild water-based cleaner, tested first
SSolvent-based cleaningDry-cleaning solvent; avoid water
WS or SWEither water or solventTest first and use the mildest option
XVacuum or brush onlyNo liquids; seek professional advice

Practical checks

  • Find the care label under the cushions or the frame.
  • Photograph it and keep it with the invoice.
  • Test any cleaner on a hidden area.
  • Call a professional when unsure.

Making the numbers work at home

Codes are guidance

Manufacturers use codes a little differently, and some labels give more detailed instructions. The label on your sofa is the authority.

No label

If you cannot find a code, ask the seller or manufacturer before using any liquid.

When in doubt

When information is missing, ask. Sellers, manufacturers and professional cleaners can usually clarify materials, construction and care, and a written answer is worth more than a guess.

A family might discover their sofa is S-coded and switch from water-based spot cleaning to the solvent a professional recommends.

The most useful habits are the small ones repeated regularly. A few minutes each week usually does more for a sofa than an occasional marathon effort or a rushed last-minute decision. Store the care label photo, invoice and your notes together; they will be useful long after delivery day.

Pitfalls worth noting

  • Relying on home remedies that the care label does not support.
  • Drying cushions in harsh direct sun without checking guidance.
  • Forgetting the frame, legs and underside during cleaning.

A simple sequence

Test first, clean gently and let everything dry fully before use. When in doubt, pause and ask a professional rather than experimenting on a visible area. Keep your measurements, swatch notes and photographs in one folder on your phone so the decision stays easy to check later.
