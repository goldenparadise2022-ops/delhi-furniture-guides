Seat Height Worksheet for 3-Seater Sofas: Elders, Kids and Tall Adults

Seat height is the dimension people notice only when it is wrong. Too low and standing up becomes an effort; too high and shorter users sit with dangling feet. Use this worksheet to record what your household actually needs from a three-seater. Everything below is practical guidance to adapt, not a set of promises about any particular product.

Comparison table

Tall adultsAround 46 cm or moreKnees rising above hips on low seats

Tick-box list

  • Measure seat height from floor to the top of the compressed cushion.
  • Test with shoes off, as most Indian homes do.
  • Record the height of the dining chair each person finds comfortable.
  • Check whether a rug will raise the floor level.

Putting it into practice

Measure compressed, not new

A cushion that measures 46 cm in a showroom may settle a little with use. Sit for a few minutes and measure again from the floor to the top of the cushion beside your thigh.

Leg height is the easiest lever

Where a model offers leg options, a small change in leg height adjusts seat height without changing the cushion. Ask whether replacement legs are available before ordering.

A note on measuring

Treat every figure here as a planning target rather than a promise. Cushion softness, arm shape, rug thickness and household habits all change how a sofa feels and fits, so measure your own room and furniture and leave a little tolerance.

A joint family in Rohini might record 47 cm for a grandparent, 43 cm for parents and note that children will manage either, pointing clearly towards the higher end of the range.

Every household is different, so adapt these suggestions rather than following them rigidly. What suits a couple in a studio may not suit a joint family, and the person who uses the sofa most should have the loudest say. Write your final numbers on a single page and carry it when you visit showrooms or compare listings online.

What to avoid

  • Using overall dimensions when the seat dimensions matter more.
  • Placing the sofa first and squeezing everything else around it.
  • Leaving no space for a side table, lamp or charging point.

How to proceed

Photograph the room from the doorway, mark the planned sofa in tape and photograph it again. Seeing the before and after side by side often makes the right size obvious. Share the notes with everyone at home before ordering, since a second opinion often catches something missed.
