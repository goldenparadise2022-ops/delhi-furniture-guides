3-Seater Sofa Width Chart for 10 x 12 ft Delhi Living Rooms

A 10 x 12 ft living room is one of the most common layouts in Delhi builder floors and DDA flats, and it is also where a three-seater is most likely to feel either perfect or oversized. This chart matches sofa widths to that specific footprint so you can see how much wall and walking space remains. The notes are written with Delhi and NCR homes in mind, though the principles apply to most Indian households.

Before you buy

  • Measure the wall between door frames, not corner to corner.
  • Subtract skirting, switchboards and AC drain pipes.
  • Tape the sofa width on the wall at seat height.
  • Check that the main door does not clip the sofa arm.

How to apply this

Why the long wall is usually right

In a 10 x 12 ft room the 12 ft wall normally faces the TV wall across the 10 ft span. That leaves a viewing distance of roughly 2.1 to 2.3 m once sofa depth and TV unit are subtracted, which suits most mid-size screens.

When to go narrower

If the room has two doors, a balcony opening or a dining table borrowing space, the extra 20 cm between a 7 ft and a 6 ft 8 in sofa often decides whether people walk around furniture or squeeze past it.

Keeping it practical

Every household is different, so adapt these suggestions rather than following them rigidly. What suits a couple in a studio may not suit a joint family, and the person who uses the sofa most should have the loudest say.

A family in a Mayur Vihar flat, for example, may find that a 2030 mm sofa leaves space for a charging side table, while a 2290 mm model forces the lamp onto the floor in front of the window.

Delhi homes vary widely, from compact DDA flats and builder floors to large NCR apartments. Measure the clear floor rather than trusting brochure areas, and note doors, switchboards, windows and AC units before you shortlist anything. Share the notes with everyone at home before ordering, since a second opinion often catches something missed.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Measuring the room once, in a hurry, and trusting the number.
  • Forgetting door swings, switchboards and the AC drain pipe.
  • Choosing the largest sofa the wall can hold instead of the one the room can breathe around.

Suggested order of work

Measure twice on different days, compare with a sofa you already know, then tape the footprint on the floor. Only after the room passes that test should you start comparing specific sizes and styles. Store the care label photo, invoice and your notes together; they will be useful long after delivery day.

Final recommendation

Mark two or three candidate widths on the wall with different tape colours and live with them for a weekend before deciding.
