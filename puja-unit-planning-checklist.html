Puja Unit Planning Checklist for Indian Apartments

Many apartments have no separate puja room, so a wall-mounted or floor-standing puja unit becomes the family's prayer space. Good planning covers size, ventilation for diyas and incense, storage for puja items and safe distances from curtains. Follow your family's traditions for placement; this guide covers the practical side.

Planning checklist

  • Decide whether family members sit on the floor or stand while praying; set the altar height to match.
  • Keep diyas and incense at least 30–45 cm below any shelf above and away from curtains.
  • Choose a heat-resistant top such as stone, metal tray or tile inlay under the diya.
  • Include a drawer for matchboxes, cotton wicks, incense and prayer books.
  • Plan a nearby socket if you use electric diyas or a small light.
  • Ensure airflow: perforated jaali panels help smoke escape from closed units.

Materials

Solid wood and marble are traditional choices. Engineered wood with a laminate can work if the diya area is protected by a heat-proof base. Brass and copper accents are easy to polish before festivals.

Common mistakes

  • Placing the unit under a low cabinet where heat and soot collect.
  • No storage, so puja items spread across the kitchen counter.
  • Closed doors without ventilation.
