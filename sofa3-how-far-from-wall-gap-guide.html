How Far Should a 3-Seater Sofa Sit From the Wall? A Gap Guide

Pushing a three-seater tight to the wall seems to save space, but a small gap can help with cleaning, air circulation and wall protection. This guide compares common gaps. Keep a pen handy: the table and checklist are meant to be filled in and compared, not just read.

Comparison table

GapBenefitConsideration
0 to 2 cmMaximum floor spaceScuffed paint; dust build-up
5 to 8 cmProtects the wall; eases cleaningSlight loss of floor space
10 to 15 cmAirflow for damp wallsVisible gap
FloatingAccess all aroundNeeds a larger room

Tick-box list

  • Check the wall for damp or seepage.
  • Use felt pads to stop sliding.
  • Check sockets and switches behind the sofa.
  • Consider curtain clearance on window walls.

Putting it into practice

Damp walls

External walls in some buildings stay damp during the monsoon. A gap lets air circulate and keeps the sofa back dry.

Wall protection

Sofa backs rub against paint over time, especially on sofas that people drop into. A small gap protects the wall.

Using the figures sensibly

Numbers are most useful when you test them. Mark outlines with painter's tape, sit on similar furniture you already own and write down what feels right, so you compare real sofas against your own notes instead of memory.

A family in an older building might keep a 10 cm gap during the monsoon and reduce it in winter.

Treat every figure here as a planning target rather than a promise. Cushion softness, arm shape, rug thickness and household habits all change how a sofa feels and fits, so measure your own room and furniture and leave a little tolerance. Write your final numbers on a single page and carry it when you visit showrooms or compare listings online.

Pitfalls worth noting

  • Copying a layout from a photo taken in a much larger room.
  • Blocking a window, balcony door or cupboard that is used daily.
  • Skipping the tape-on-the-floor test because the numbers 'look fine'.

A simple sequence

Start with a rough sketch, add the fixed elements such as doors and windows, then pencil in the sofa. Tape the outline on the floor, walk every route with something in your hands and adjust before you shortlist models. Share the notes with everyone at home before ordering, since a second opinion often catches something missed.

Next steps

Try a small gap for a week and see whether the room feels any different; most people do not notice it.
