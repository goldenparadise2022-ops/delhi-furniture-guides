3-Seater Sofa Depth Planner: 80 cm vs 90 cm vs 100 cm

Width gets most of the attention, but depth decides how a three-seater feels to sit on and how much floor it takes from the room. This planner compares three common overall depths and what each one means for posture and circulation. Use it alongside your own measurements and household routines rather than as a fixed rulebook.

Planning grid

Overall depthTypical sitting feelFloor impact
About 80 cmUpright, feet flat for most adultsLeaves the most walkway in narrow rooms
About 90 cmBalanced; room to lean back with a cushionWorks in most 10 ft wide rooms
About 100 cmLounging; shorter users may need back pillowsNeeds a generous room or open plan

Checklist

  • Measure the depth of a chair or sofa you already find comfortable.
  • Note the seat depth separately from the overall depth.
  • Subtract sofa depth from room width to find the remaining walkway.
  • Ask who sits longest on the sofa and design for them.

What the table does not show

Overall depth versus seat depth

Overall depth includes the back frame and cushions; seat depth is the usable cushion from front edge to back cushion. Two sofas with the same overall depth can sit very differently, so ask for both numbers.

Household height matters

Shorter family members often prefer a shallower seat so their knees bend at the front edge. Taller members may like deeper seats. Loose back cushions can bridge the gap if the household is mixed.

Adapting to your room

Room shape, light and the way people move through the space matter as much as any single dimension. Sketch the room roughly to scale and pencil in the sofa, tables and walking routes before making a decision.

In a household where grandparents read the newspaper each morning and teenagers sprawl in the evening, a 90 cm depth with firm back cushions is often a reasonable compromise.

Numbers are most useful when you test them. Mark outlines with painter's tape, sit on similar furniture you already own and write down what feels right, so you compare real sofas against your own notes instead of memory. Keep your measurements, swatch notes and photographs in one folder on your phone so the decision stays easy to check later.

Mistakes that are easy to make

  • Planning the sofa in isolation from the centre table and TV unit.
  • Ignoring the route people take from the entrance to the kitchen.
  • Assuming showroom proportions will look the same in a smaller room.

Step-by-step approach

Decide what the room is mainly for, list the furniture it must hold, and give the sofa its place last. This order stops the sofa from quietly taking space that a walkway or side table needs. A short written record of what you chose and why makes future cleaning, repairs or replacements much simpler.

Before you order

Sit on your current sofa with a measuring tape and record where your back and knees land; that single number usually narrows the choice.
